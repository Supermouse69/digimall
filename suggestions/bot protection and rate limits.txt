Title: Bot protection and rate limits (Phase 2f)
Date: 2026-10-31
Priority: High
Status: APPROVED + BUILT 2026-10-31: Turnstile on all 5 forms, counters in MongoDB (option A). CSRF/CORS (point 3) postponed until deploy.
Area: security / infra / cost

What's the problem?
Our sign-up, login, forgot-password and resend-email forms can be hit by bots as fast as they like.
Payload already locks ONE account after 5 wrong passwords, but nothing stops:
- one bot trying passwords on thousands of different emails
- mass fake sign-ups
- spamming "forgot password" / "resend email" to flood someone's inbox (and burn our email quota
  once a real email service is connected)

Why it matters (what could go wrong):
Account takeovers, fake accounts, our email sender getting blocked as spam, higher costs.

What I suggest:
1. Cloudflare Turnstile ("I'm not a robot" check, usually invisible) on: sign-up, login,
   forgot password, resend verification email, and the hidden admin login.
   The server checks every Turnstile token with Cloudflare. A missing or bad token means the form is refused.
   - Development uses Cloudflare's official TEST keys (always pass), so no account is needed yet.
   - Before going live: create a free Turnstile widget in the Cloudflare dashboard for digimall.online.

2. Rate limits (max tries per time window), per IP address, and per email where it makes sense.
   Proposed starting numbers (easy to change, all in one file):
   - login: 10 tries / 15 min per IP
   - sign-up: 5 / hour per IP
   - forgot password: 5 / hour per IP and 3 / hour per email
   - resend verification: already 1 / minute per account; add 5 / hour per IP
   - admin login: 5 / 15 min per IP (on top of the existing 2FA lock)
   Later phases reuse the same helper for chat, checkout and rider requests.
   They also cover Payload's own login endpoint (/api/users/login, used by /admin/login for sellers)
   through a Payload hook. Otherwise a bot could skip our forms and go straight to the API.

   Where the counters are stored (in-memory doesn't work on Vercel):
   A) MongoDB (the database we already have). A small "rate-limits" collection whose old entries
      delete themselves. Free, no new account, no new package. A bit slower than Redis, which is
      fine for beta traffic.
   B) Upstash Redis. Built for this and very fast, with a free tier. Needs a new account,
      2 new env vars and 2 new packages (@upstash/ratelimit, @upstash/redis).
   Either way it sits behind one module, so we can switch later without touching the forms.

3. Payload CSRF + CORS limited to our own site (CLAUDE.md §10.4): only NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL
   (+ localhost in development) may send logged-in requests to /api.

Effort and cost: Free. About one work session. Turnstile is free. Option A costs nothing extra.
Upstash has a free tier.

Trade-offs / risks:
- Turnstile loads a script from Cloudflare (challenges.cloudflare.com). Cloudflare sees the visitor's IP
  for the check. Mention it in the Privacy Notice (you write that).
- Strict limits can block real users who share one IP (same household Wi-Fi, or mobile data
  carriers sharing IPs). The numbers above are generous for that reason.
- CORS/CSRF misconfigured = Payload admin stops working. Easy to undo (one config change).

Rollback: every piece is one commit. Remove the env vars / revert the commit to turn it off.

What I need from you:
- Yes/no to Turnstile on those 5 forms
- A (MongoDB) or B (Upstash) for the counters
- OK to add env vars NEXT_PUBLIC_TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY and TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY (test keys in dev)
- OK to set Payload csrf/cors in payload.config.ts
